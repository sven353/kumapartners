    The invisible runway killer: artificial harmony & the silent veto

    Elevated risk: silent veto & mandate ambiguity detected

    Friction does not always show up as open confrontation. In high-stakes leadership teams the most expensive drag is silent: strategic initiatives nodded through in Monday meetings that quietly stall in corridor execution.

    When co-founder friendships or early loyalties blur executive mandates (CEO, CTO, CPO), the team avoids holding peers accountable to protect personal harmony. The cost of avoiding principled tension is paid out of company velocity and, in the end, out of runway.

    Why this matters commercially

    Structural failure risk

    Harvard Business School research found that 65% of high-potential start-ups fail as a result of conflict among the founders.

    Harvard Business School, Noam Wasserman, The Founder’s Dilemmas (2012)

    Runway and velocity dilution

    In a McKinsey survey of 1,228 respondents, 61% said most of their organisation’s decision-making time is used ineffectively. That time is paid for out of runway.

    McKinsey & Company, Decision making in the age of urgency (2019)

        The executive perception gap

        Most leadership teams believe they are aligned on priorities. In our work with scale-stage teams, founders, COOs and functional VPs tend to feel friction in different places, even when they agree in the room. A self-assessment from one leader exposes the symptom. Calibrating across the whole leadership team shows where the structural divide sits.

        The next step: the Kuma 14-Day Leadership Friction Scan
        1. Confidential 1-on-1 executive interviews. Diagnostic conversations with each executive to isolate unstated friction and silent vetoes, mapped into a cross-functional friction heat map.
        2. Mandate clarity. Clear authority boundaries between the CEO, CTO, CPO and go-to-market leads.
        3. Debrief and 90-day alignment compact. Type 1 and Type 2 decision rights and a zero-passive-veto operating agreement, agreed in a facilitated session.

        Identifying friction is step one. Dismantling it requires external leverage.

        You cannot untangle a silent veto from inside the room. When co-founders are also close friends, direct confrontation feels like a personal attack, leading to polite paralysis. Dismantling this requires an external, neutral operating framework to separate personal loyalty from executive execution.
