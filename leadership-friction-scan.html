        The executive perception gap

        Most leadership teams believe they are aligned on priorities. In our work with scale-stage teams, founders, COOs and functional VPs tend to feel friction in different places, even when they agree in the room. A self-assessment from one leader exposes the symptom. Calibrating across the whole leadership team shows where the structural divide sits.

        The next step: the Kuma 14-Day Leadership Friction Scan
        1. Confidential 1-on-1 executive interviews. Diagnostic conversations with each executive to isolate unstated friction and silent vetoes, mapped into a cross-functional friction heat map.
        2. Mandate clarity. Clear authority boundaries between the CEO, CTO, CPO and go-to-market leads.
        3. Debrief and 90-day alignment compact. Type 1 and Type 2 decision rights and a zero-passive-veto operating agreement, agreed in a facilitated session.

        Identifying friction is step one. Dismantling it requires external leverage.

        You cannot untangle a silent veto from inside the room. When co-founders are also close friends, direct operational challenge feels like a personal attack. Dismantling this requires an external, neutral operating framework to separate personal loyalty from executive execution.

        Kuma Operating Framework • Reference

        The Anatomy of Leadership Friction in Scale-Stage Ventures

        Developed by Sven Mulfinger and the partners at Kuma Partners, the Leadership Friction Scan measures the gap between executive intent and what the organisation actually does. As a company grows, the cost of coordinating its leaders tends to become a larger limit on speed than the market itself.

        Read the four vectors, definitions and sources

        1. The Silent Veto and artificial harmony

        Decisions agreed in the executive meeting are quietly eroded outside it. When co-founder friendships blur executive mandates (CEO, CTO, CPO), the team trades peer accountability for polite comfort, and speed suffers.

        2. Founder centrality drag

        The founders remain the clearing house for reversible operational calls. Execution speed defaults to the founders’ calendar, and initiatives stall while they wait for sign-off.

        3. Defensive silos

        Functions retreat into local targets and optimise for their own metrics rather than company outcomes. Activity stays high while unified progress stays low.

        4. Executive energy and bandwidth burn

        Leaders spend more energy on internal politics, consensus loops and meeting churn than on the market, which breeds cynicism and fatigue.

        Definitions

        What is the Silent Veto in executive teams?

        The Silent Veto happens when a decision agreed inside the executive meeting is quietly undermined outside the room through passive compliance, delayed execution or corridor pushback. It is the clearest symptom of artificial harmony in a leadership team.

        How does founder centrality create leadership friction?

        Founder centrality drag happens when reversible operational decisions default inward to the CEO or co-founders. The leadership team becomes a queue, execution speed is capped at the founders’ calendar, and decision latency spreads across every function.

        What is the 12 to 60 Leadership Friction Index?

        The Kuma Leadership Friction Index scores twelve statements from 1 to 5 across four vectors: Decision Velocity & Autonomy, Strategic Cohesion vs. Defensive Silos, Cognitive Bandwidth & Energy Drain, and Principled Candor vs. The Silent Veto. Totals run from 12 to 60: High-Flow Velocity (12 to 25), Operational Drag (26 to 42) and Critical Systemic Gridlock (43 to 60).
