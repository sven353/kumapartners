Strategic planning for CEOs and co-founders: setting direction without burning out

By Dr. Sven Mulfinger · Published October 2026

A strategy retreat built around founder stamina is two to three days in which the CEO and co-founders restore their capacity before they commit capital and roadmap for the next twelve to twenty-four months. The order matters. Depleted leaders default to defensive, risk-averse or incremental compromises. When clarity and trust come back first, the strategic decisions are bolder and they last.

What founder depletion costs the business

In fast-growing companies, founder exhaustion is often worn as a badge of honour and rarely managed as a business risk. It carries a real cost all the same.

The numbers are hard to ignore. In a survey of more than 250 C-suite executives, presented by Calm at Fortune’s Brainstorm AI conference in December 2025, half said they had thought about stepping down. And in a McKinsey survey of 1,228 managers (“Decision making in the age of urgency”, 2019), just over half said they spend more than 30% of their working time on decisions, while 61% said most of that time is used ineffectively.

Tired leaders make that worse. Under sustained pressure, decision quality drops, risk aversion rises and choices that should take a week drag on for a quarter.

For co-founders the risk doubles, because individual fatigue turns into friction between partners. Noam Wasserman of Harvard Business School reports that about 65% of high-potential startups fail because of conflict among co-founders. Depletion is often where that conflict starts:

  • Decisions drag on as partners avoid contentious trade-offs to save their energy.
  • Unspoken resentment about workload, contribution or focus hardens into separate camps.
  • Planning becomes a tired negotiation between two people looking for short-term relief, instead of a clear look at where the capital should go.

Separating recovery from strategy is a governance mistake. Clear heads are the condition for sound commercial judgement, which is why this protocol starts there.

The four-phase horizon protocol

Phase 1: decompression (the first half-day)

Nobody moves from firefighting to long-range thinking in fifteen minutes. The retreat starts by taking the adrenaline out:

  • No devices in the sessions: laptops closed, notifications off, no slides.
  • A real change of place: a private, secluded estate such as a historic Catalan masia, with space around it.
  • A low-key start: an unhurried dinner, a walk on the estate and informal conversation, so everyone can step out of emergency mode.

This phase has one job: turn down the noise so that a longer view can return.

Phase 2: the partnership and energy audit (day 1, morning)

Before anyone opens a spreadsheet, the co-founders look at the health of the partnership itself. With a mediator who has run a company, each founder answers four questions alone and then together:

  1. Energy: on an honest scale of 1 to 10, how much capacity do you have right now?
  2. Drains: which of your responsibilities create real leverage for the business, and which drain your focus and create bottlenecks?
  3. The unspoken list: which disagreements, equity assumptions or habits have we been avoiding in the boardroom?
  4. Assumptions: which beliefs about our market, runway or team from twelve months ago are no longer true?

Saying these things in a setting with no political exposure clears the backlog that otherwise slows every decision.

Phase 3: horizon labs and trade-offs (day 1 afternoon to day 2 morning)

With clearer heads and less friction, the planning itself can be rigorous:

  • Scenarios three to five years out: testing the business model against technology shifts, advances in AI and tighter capital markets.
  • Capital and runway: where to invest disproportionately to win, and which secondary initiatives stop now.
  • Roles: redrawing the founders’ lanes for the company’s next stage, so neither is stuck in a role built for an earlier one.

Phase 4: a compact the founders can sustain (the final half-day)

Strategic clarity collapses if the founders go back to an unsustainable way of working on Monday. The last half-day turns the decisions into a signed founder compact, modelled on the 90-day compact, that records:

  • Decision rights: who decides on product, commercial strategy, capital and hiring, with one owner each.
  • A personal stop list: what the CEO and co-founders will delegate or drop straight away.
  • Protected thinking time: fixed slots that operational work is not allowed to eat.
  • Review dates: day 30, 60 and 90 check-ins on whether the compact is holding.

Five signs your partnership needs this

Consider a founder strategy retreat when:

  1. You have been in non-stop execution for more than twelve months without stepping back to look at the bigger picture.
  2. You and your co-founder disagree more often, or have started avoiding difficult conversations to keep the peace.
  3. Big decisions on capital, strategy or structure keep sliding from one quarter to the next.
  4. You are working longer hours for less effect and more frustration.
  5. One of you has quietly thought about stepping back from running the company.

Why the facilitator should be a former operator

Co-founders rarely open up to someone who has never carried a P&L, watched the runway shrink or handled a difficult board. Advice from that position tends to ring hollow.

A founder retreat needs someone in the room who:

  • Has sat in the top chair and knows the weight of scaling under venture or private equity pressure.
  • Can stay neutral, break circular debates between founders and name evasion without damaging the partnership.
  • Keeps the retreat honest about both the founders’ capacity and the commercial reality.

Common questions

Why should founder recovery come before setting strategy?

Depleted leaders default to defensive, risk-averse or incremental compromises. Restoring perspective and trust first leads to bolder and more durable strategic decisions.

How is a founder strategy retreat different from a wellness retreat?

A wellness retreat ends when everyone goes home. A strategy retreat deals with energy and relationships because they shape the quality of decisions, and it ends with clear decision rights, agreed trade-offs and a signed compact.

Who should attend a founder strategy retreat?

The CEO and the co-founders, usually two to six people. Keeping the group this small gives the confidentiality needed to discuss equity, workload and governance openly.
