Company offsites for distributed teams: 15 to 100 people without forced fun

By Dr. Sven Mulfinger · Published October 2026

Once a distributed company grows past about twenty people, getting everyone together takes more design than booking a resort and hoping the conversations happen.

Remote work gives a company access to talent, and it costs the small social signals people pick up in an office. So when the whole company finally meets, leaders tend to overcorrect. Some fill three days with keynote slides. Others run an unstructured party with an open bar and icebreakers. People go home tired from both, and little has moved.

A good company offsite gives the full group a shared direction, gives each team time to work on its own problems and plans the social side with the same care as the agenda.

How should the days be structured?

Keeping fifty or a hundred people in one room for three days wears everyone out. Run two tracks.

  • Full-group sessions, kept short. The whole company meets in a plenary space such as the theatre at Norrsken House in Poblenou. The founders set out the strategy, the market and the operating principles, so every function hears the same thing.
  • Small working groups for most of the time. Product, engineering and commercial teams split into pods and work on bottlenecks scoped in advance. A senior operator steers each pod, and each one leaves with a plan owned by its department lead.

Above 20 participants we put two principal facilitators in the room. One moderates the full-group discussion while the other works with the pods.

Element Conventional company retreat Two-track company offsite
In the room Everyone in rows, listening to presentations Short full-group briefings alternating with working pods
Social time Compulsory games, trust falls or quiz nights Cooking over an open fire, a sail from Port Vell
Drinks Late nights at the hotel bar Good food, with non-alcoholic pairings of the same standard
Planning Carried by the People lead or Chief of Staff Handled end to end: transport, AV, venue and dietary needs

How do you build connection without forced fun?

Trust does not come from exercises people are told to enjoy. It forms when colleagues from different teams spend unhurried time together doing something real.

In Catalonia we build that time around the place.

  • Cooking over an open fire. The whole company gathers in a masia courtyard with private chefs and prepares the meal together. Cooking side by side flattens hierarchy quickly.
  • Time on the water. A chartered catamaran leaves Port Vell with phones put away. A hard debate from the afternoon often settles on its own out there.
  • Evenings that work for everyone. Teams include people who do not drink, neurodivergent colleagues and a range of dietary needs. Wine tastings come with botanical pairings and kombuchas of the same quality, and menus follow dietary profiles collected before arrival.

How do you protect your People team?

The hidden cost of a large offsite falls on your own staff. Heads of People and Chiefs of Staff can lose weeks to coach bookings, hotel blocks and AV lists.

We take that work off them: transfers from Barcelona-El Prat, hotel blocks, checked connectivity, dietary planning and stage AV. Your People lead can then take part as a leader of the company while someone else runs the event.

If your Head of People wants a role in the room, we plan the split in advance. They lead the cultural and functional sessions and we take the commercial trade-offs.
