The Premise

The higher the seat, the fewer people can be honest with you in it.

01

The isolation is structural

Your board wants confidence. Your team wants certainty. Your peers outside the company can't see the whole picture. There is often no one inside the system who can tell you the truth without a stake in your answer.

02

The calendar never volunteers the time

Reflection loses to operational fires by default. Left alone, a leader's schedule will always choose the urgent over the important. It has to be built by someone else, or it doesn't happen.

03

Generic reflection isn't the gap

You don't need another sounding board or a facilitated workshop. You need operators who have held the seat themselves, in a room built for the specific weight of running something.

The Work

Three days, three levels of the same question.

Kuma Sanctuary moves deliberately between the person carrying the role, the professional practicing it, and the enterprise depending on it. Each is treated as its own subject, not a warm-up for the next.

Pillar One

The Person

A cognitive reset away from the operating rhythm that has likely run unchecked for years. Nervous-system restoration, unhurried self-reflection, and the kind of quiet that a full calendar rarely allows.

Pillar Two

The Professional

Blind spots surfaced by peers who carry equivalent weight elsewhere, not by consultants observing from outside it. Direct, private challenge on how you actually lead, not how you describe leading.

Pillar Three

The Enterprise

The long-view questions that rarely survive a quarterly cycle. Capital allocation, board dynamics, succession, and the resilience decisions that are easiest to defer and costliest to.

The Cohort

Every seat is vetted. That is what protects the room.

  • 01

    Confidentiality, without exception

    The Chatham House Rule governs every conversation. What is said in the room stays in the room, and is never attributed outside it.

  • 02

    No vendors, no pitches

    This is not a origination channel for anyone in the room, Kuma included. No selling, no soliciting, no exchange of business under the pretense of peer conversation.

  • 03

    Deliberate composition

    Each cohort is built by hand for non-competing sectors, comparable stakes, and genuine peer standing, not simply seniority or company size.

  • 04

    Senior facilitation throughout

    The room is led by Kuma partners who have run a P&L and sat in the seat themselves, not professional facilitators observing from the sidelines.

The Days

Structured enough to hold the work. Open enough to breathe.

There is no minute-by-minute schedule. Executives at this level don't need one, and a packed itinerary works against the purpose. What follows is the shape of the four days, not a script.

Day One

Arrival and unhurried grounding

Private arrival through the afternoon and evening. No sessions, no introductions to perform. Dinner together, off the record, with the first quiet stretch of unscheduled time most participants have had in months.

Day Two

Closed-door advisory circles

Small-group sessions where each executive brings one real, unresolved decision to the room and leaves with the peer challenge and clarity that no internal conversation could offer. Long, unstructured breaks between.

Day Three

Strategic architecture and personal equilibrium

The morning is held for private, unhurried solo reflection and unstructured movement across the alpine grounds. The afternoon moves into deep enterprise stress-testing, closed-door deconstruction of capital allocation, succession, and governance resilience. The day concludes with an extended private dinner.

Day Four

Departure with something to carry

A closing circle, not a summary deck. Each participant leaves with a small number of private commitments to themselves, not a slide to bring back to the office.
